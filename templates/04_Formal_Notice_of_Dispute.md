### 1. Statutory Demand for Records (47 U.S.C. § 551(d))
Pursuant to the **Cable Communications Policy Act, 47 U.S.C. § 551(d)**, a subscriber has an explicit statutory right to inspect all personally identifiable information, transaction records, and account data collected and maintained by the operator. 

Demand is hereby made that Comcast produce complete, unredacted copies of the following within thirty (30) days:
1. The full electronic audit trail, timestamped system logs, SMS gateway delivery receipts, session identifiers, and IP logs regarding the alleged contract assent for telephone number ending in [INSERT_LAST_4_DIGITS] on or about [INSERT_ENROLLMENT_MONTH/YEAR].
2. The exact electronic disclosures, links, and contractual terms rendered to the subscriber at the time of the alleged verification.
3. Complete billing ledgers reflecting the charge-off and debt assignment history.

Under **47 U.S.C. § 551(f)**, failure to provide subscriber access as mandated by federal law authorizes a civil action in United States District Court for liquidated damages of $100 per day (or $1,000, whichever is greater), plus reasonable attorneys' fees and costs.

### 2. Contract Unenforceability (15 U.S.C. § 7001)
Under the **Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (15 U.S.C. § 7001(d)-(e))**, an electronic contract is enforceable **only if** the record is retained in a form capable of accurate reproduction for later reference by all parties. 

Internal, non-reproducible database flags do not satisfy the E-SIGN Act. If Comcast cannot produce the underlying auditable electronic consent record, no enforceable term contract exists, and the ETF constitutes an unlawful charge.
